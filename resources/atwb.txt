Granddad had obviously played these things to death, man. Some of the discs were worn and scratched, and the little inlays were torn and faded… he loved these albums, man.

So anyway, my dad can’t play these discs and neither can I, no-one we know can, so I just take them home and check them out when I’m having a beer… you know, read the inlays and that, read the lyrics, get to know who played guitar in Extreme and who sang for Little Angels… and I start to obsess over these things.

Then, get this, about a month later my buddy Bob Solid gets a job as a teacher in a nearby school, which is hilarious. If you knew Bob you would NOT let him into a school, never mind have him teaching anything, but he is handy with technology and fixing stuff which helped him get the job I guess. Anyway, he’s been there a week or two and then he has to give a lesson on historical technology, and that weekend he’s telling me about it in the pub, and he says “Oh yeah, and we have all these ancient music players in the storeroom, like from before holotapes? There’s players for these huge reels of magnetic tape, gizmos that will play wax cylinders, all kinds of disc players and readers…” and I cut him off right there. I whipped out one of my granddad’s discs from my pocket – “Slave to the Grind” by Skid Row, in fact – and said “Wait, can you play these things?” and Bob says “Yep, definitely!”

Well Bob’s a genius when it comes to connecting things to other things, and within a few hours we had a large chunk of my granddad’s music collection on holotape. Man that was a night.

Next day I went over to dad’s house and told him all about it, and played him a couple of tracks… but he did NOT dig it. He said “Tommi, if I want to listen to that crap I’ll put on an oldies station. I found that box and I knew you would get a kick out of it, but it’s not for me. It’s music of the past. It was my father’s thing, not mine.” Huh.

Naturally my interest was piqued, being a music guy, and I told him I’d always wanted to be on the radio. I told him about the discovery of my granddad’s music collection, we split a bottle of wine, and cooked up the idea for “As The World Burns”. So now, here I am every Saturday night from 10pm, playing you the rock music from my dead granddad’s loft. Music that makes me smile in a world that… really doesn’t. 

I hope you get a kick out of it. I sure do. And if you don’t… well… that radio dial is yours to command. You dig?
