# XXX — Westminster's Transformation

## Discussion

Westminster is going to physically become female.

The transformation is intended to be a **complete physical/biological rewrite**, rather than cosmetic transition or conventional surgery. The setting is Fallout 4, approximately 2287, so we are not constrained by the capabilities of real-world medicine in 2026.

The desired result is that post-transformation Westminster is physiologically female in every meaningful biological respect:

- no Y chromosome;
- female reproductive anatomy;
- female endocrine system;
- no Adam's apple;
- no facial/skull structure resulting from testosterone-driven puberty;
- appropriate female skeletal, muscular, fat-distribution and other physiological characteristics;
- essentially a ground-up reconstruction of the body.

However, Westminster remains the same individual and the same physiological age.

An important desired detail is **physical continuity**. If pre-transformation Westminster has, for example, a scar on his forehead, post-transformation Westminster should have the same scar on her female face. The machine therefore should not simply produce a generic female body; it should reconstruct *Westminster*.

The transformation should also take several days. The player must leave Westminster at the location where it occurs and return a few days later to find the transformation complete.

## XXX should be a machine

The current working idea is that XXX is some sort of machine/pod/chamber.

The existing asset for the Kowalski protection shelter may potentially be reusable for the facility/chamber.

The key design question was: **why does such a machine exist in the Fallout universe?**

Fallout 4 does not, as far as we know, establish an existing piece of technology specifically designed for gender transformation. We therefore should not invent a conveniently named "gender-changing machine" that exists solely to service this plot.

### Core solution

XXX should instead be a **pre-War experimental human biological reconstruction system**.

Its original purpose was not to change sex.

It was designed to reconstruct human beings who had suffered catastrophic physical damage — potentially:

- severe burns;
- radiation damage;
- chemical contamination;
- battlefield trauma;
- massive organ loss;
- severe congenital abnormalities;
- or other injuries that conventional medicine could not repair.

The machine's underlying technology is essentially:

> **construct a complete living human organism from a comprehensive biological specification.**

It does not merely replace individual organs. It can take the organism apart and rebuild it while preserving the individual's identity.

This makes Westminster's transformation an application of the machine's general capability rather than its special purpose.

### Why this fits Fallout

The machine can be an obscure branch of pre-War biotechnology, existing within the technological envelope established by Fallout.

Fallout's pre-War/Institute technology already supports extraordinary biological engineering. The Institute's synthetic-organic research and Gen 3 synths demonstrate that the setting permits technology vastly beyond contemporary real-world medicine.

XXX therefore does not need to be Institute technology.

In fact, it is probably better **not** to make it an Institute machine, because that immediately raises the question of why the Institute did not make wider use of it.

Instead:

- XXX is pre-War;
- it may have been a military or classified medical research project;
- it was experimental;
- the people who understood it died in the Great War;
- the facility and machine survived;
- almost nobody in 2287 knows what it actually does.

This is deliberately Fallout-ish: extraordinary pre-War technology survives while the knowledge required to operate or understand it is largely lost.

## The machine is not "a sex-change machine"

This distinction is important.

In dialogue, characters should probably never call it a "sex-change machine."

Possible in-universe descriptions include:

- reconstruction chamber;
- restoration unit;
- bioforge;
- the chamber;
- the machine.

The machine does not have "change sex" as its defining function.

It reconstructs a person according to a biological specification, and sex is one of the parameters that can be reconstructed.

Therefore it can rebuild:

- chromosomes;
- gonads;
- reproductive anatomy;
- endocrine system;
- skeletal structure;
- musculature;
- larynx;
- facial structure;
- skin;
- hair follicles;
- organs;
- etc.

The Y chromosome genuinely disappears because the new body is not merely an edited version of the old one.

## Preserving Westminster

The machine should reconstruct **Westminster herself**, not generate a generic female body.

The scar example is particularly useful.

If Westminster enters with a scar on her forehead, the scar remains after reconstruction because it is part of her physical identity/history.

The machine therefore has some sort of comprehensive biological model/template of the individual.

The transformation is consequently better understood as:

> **Westminster's body being reconstructed as the body of the woman she is.**

rather than:

> a male body being cosmetically changed into a female body.

This also gives the transformation emotional/narrative weight.

## Why does it take several days?

The process should be deliberately non-instantaneous.

A possible conceptual sequence:

### Day 0 — Calibration

Westminster enters the chamber.

The machine performs an extraordinarily comprehensive scan and constructs its biological model of her.

### Days 1–2 — Reconstruction

The machine performs the physical reconstruction:

- cellular reconstruction;
- skeletal reconstruction;
- organ generation/replacement;
- endocrine restructuring;
- neural protection;
- etc.

### Days 3–4 — Integration

The reconstructed body must become a coherent functioning organism.

The machine stabilises:

- hormonal systems;
- immune system;
- metabolism;
- neurological integration;
- and other physiological systems.

### Final period — Recovery

Westminster remains unconscious or heavily sedated while the machine waits for her biological parameters to stabilise.

The machine therefore cannot simply release her immediately.

This gives the quest a natural structure:

1. Westminster enters XXX.
2. The player receives instructions to leave her.
3. The player leaves the facility.
4. Several in-game days pass.
5. The player returns.
6. Westminster emerges transformed.

The actual transformation therefore happens largely off-screen.

The player experiences:

> **before → absence → return → after**

rather than watching a lengthy medical transformation sequence.

## Why hasn't everyone in the Commonwealth used XXX?

The machine should probably have **never successfully performed this process in ordinary circumstances**.

Possible reasons:

- it was a prototype;
- it was experimental;
- the research team died before completing human trials;
- the documentation is incomplete;
- the operating procedure was lost;
- it has been dormant for 210 years;
- very few people even know the facility exists.

Westminster may therefore be the first person in centuries to use it successfully.

This avoids creating the problem that a functioning transformation machine should logically already be widespread.

## The mystic

A major remaining question is:

> **How does the mystic know about XXX?**

The preferred direction is that the mystic should not secretly be a scientist who understands the machine's technology.

Instead, she knows **where Westminster needs to go**, without necessarily understanding exactly how the machine works.

She might possess:

- a surviving fragment of information;
- a location;
- a name;
- a symbol;
- a piece of pre-War documentation;
- or some other old clue.

She may simply know:

> "There is a place where bodies can be remade."

This preserves the mystic's existing narrative role.

She understands what Westminster needs without becoming an omniscient technical expert.

## The major narrative function of XXX

XXX is not merely the mechanism by which Westminster changes sex.

It needs to complete the larger story architecture.

Westminster has spent the story moving backwards:

- body;
- medicine;
- genes;
- Vault 81;
- family;
- ancestry;
- Tommi;
- the past.

He has been trying to answer:

> **"What happened to me?"**

The recognition/revelation establishes that the real problem is internal: Westminster has to recognise himself/herself.

But the story does not end at recognition.

The mystic then points Westminster toward XXX.

XXX therefore represents the transition:

> **recognition → agency**

Westminster has finally understood who she is.

Now she has to *do something*.

The physical transformation should therefore feel like the culmination of that journey rather than a random sci-fi contrivance added to the ending.

## Current provisional conception

**XXX is a pre-War experimental human biological reconstruction system.**

Its intended purpose was to reconstruct catastrophically injured humans from a comprehensive biological template.

It survived the Great War in a sealed/forgotten facility.

Its technology is consistent with the extraordinary biological engineering capabilities established elsewhere in Fallout.

It can completely reconstruct Westminster's body while preserving her individual biological identity.

The machine does not "turn a man into a woman" as a dedicated function.

**It rebuilds Westminster as the woman she is.**

The process takes several days because it involves complete organism-level reconstruction and subsequent biological stabilisation.

The player must leave Westminster at the facility and return later.

The machine is obscure because it is an unfinished/forgotten pre-War prototype whose operators and knowledge base were lost.

The mystic knows enough to direct Westminster there, but does not necessarily understand the technical workings of the machine.

## Remaining design questions

The next discussion should attack these questions:

1. Who built XXX?
2. What was the precise original purpose of the research programme?
3. Where is XXX located?
4. What does the facility look like?
5. How does the mystic know about it?
6. What does Westminster know before entering?
7. What does the player know?
8. What does the machine actually look/sound like?
9. What happens when the player returns?
10. How does Westminster's transformed appearance communicate continuity with the person who entered?
11. How does this connect to the final story/quest structure?
12. Whether the Kowalski protection shelter asset can plausibly be reused for the chamber/facility.

The central principle to preserve is:

> **XXX is not a magic gender-change device. It is a forgotten pre-War machine capable of reconstructing a human being from a biological specification — and Westminster is using it to become physically what she has finally recognised herself to be.**
