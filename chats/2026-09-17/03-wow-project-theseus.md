# Session Capture — Project Theseus / Westminster
## Working notes from this evening

This is a raw capture of the salient ideas and decisions from the evening's discussion. It is intentionally not yet reorganised into CURRENT-STATE.md, ADRs, quest documentation, or implementation notes. Treat the explicit "locked/settled" items as decisions; treat the remaining ideas as material to develop.

---

# 1. Core XXX concept

The previously generic placeholder "XXX" is now **Project Theseus**.

The current settled concept is:

> **XXX is not a magic gender-change device. It is a forgotten pre-War machine capable of reconstructing a human being from a biological specification — and Westminster is using it to become physically what she has finally recognised herself to be.**

Project Theseus is a pre-War West-Tek / U.S. military biomedical research programme.

It was NOT originally designed as a gender-transition machine.

Its original purpose was military/medical:

- reconstruct catastrophically wounded soldiers;
- replace destroyed tissue;
- replace organs;
- reconstruct limbs;
- eventually reconstruct increasingly large portions of the human body;
- ultimately reconstruct an entire human organism.

The gender transition is an unforeseen later use of an extraordinarily advanced reconstruction technology.

This distinction is important and should remain fundamental.

---

# 2. Westminster's agency — LOCKED

Westminster **knows she is trans before entering the machine**.

She has consciously recognised the truth about herself.

The player does NOT know.

This is now considered the stronger version and is settled.

The machine does not:

- diagnose Westminster's gender;
- decide that she is a woman;
- reveal her identity to her;
- cause her to become trans;
- make the decision on her behalf.

Westminster makes the decision.

She knows what she is going into.

She knows what she wants.

She chooses it.

The player simply isn't told.

The key word that settled this was:

> **Agency.**

The transformation is something Westminster deliberately chooses, rather than something that happens to her or something the machine decides for her.

---

# 3. Player reveal — LOCKED

The player is NOT told before the procedure that this is a gender transition.

The intention is for the player to believe they are accompanying Westminster to an extraordinary piece of lost pre-War medical technology / reconstruction machinery.

The Project Theseus history can be discovered beforehand.

The player can understand what the machine was designed to do.

But the player should not be able to say:

> "Ah, obviously this is the gender-transition quest."

The actual revelation comes when Westminster emerges.

The player understands what has happened at essentially the same moment they see the result.

The important asymmetry is:

> The player doesn't know.
>
> Westminster does.

The player therefore gets a retrospective realisation:

> "Oh. THAT is what this was about."

Whereas Westminster's experience is:

> "I finally get to be me."

Avoid excessive breadcrumbs that make the twist obvious before the procedure.

There can be hindsight clues, but they should read naturally as ordinary character details until the reveal recontextualises them.

---

# 4. Westminster's transformation — LOCKED in principle

The transformation is a complete physical reconstruction, not cosmetic transition.

The Fallout setting is ~2287 and does not need to be constrained by real-world 2026 medical technology.

The machine can perform a ground-up biological reconstruction.

The post-Theseus body is physiologically female.

The transformation can include:

- no Y chromosome;
- female chromosomal configuration;
- female reproductive anatomy;
- female endocrine system;
- female skeletal structure;
- female musculature;
- female facial structure;
- female larynx / voice physiology;
- appropriate skin, hair, etc.;
- complete reconstruction of the physical body.

There is no need for:
- electrolysis;
- cosmetic surgery;
- artificial approximation;
- partial transition;
- "male skeleton but female appearance";
- any of the physical limitations of contemporary transition technology.

This is a full biological rewrite/reconstruction.

The machine is reconstructing the person, not simply applying cosmetic changes.

# 5. Physical continuity

A particularly liked idea:

If Westminster has a distinctive physical mark before the procedure, such as a scar on the forehead, the reconstructed Westminster can retain the same scar.

The point is that the machine is reconstructing **Westminster**, not manufacturing a generic woman.

The scar can therefore act as a subtle visual continuity marker:

- same person;
- completely reconstructed body;
- same history.

This should probably be retained.

# 6. Time required for the procedure — LOCKED in broad form

The procedure takes several days.

Earlier Project Theseus terminal concepts established an estimated procedure duration of roughly:

> **96–120 hours**

This is useful both narratively and technically.

Gameplay structure:

1. Westminster enters the machine.
2. The player leaves.
3. Several in-game days pass.
4. The player returns.
5. Westminster emerges.

The actual transformation does not need to be shown.

This also gives us a natural reason for the player to leave the location rather than standing around waiting.

# 7. Westminster's emotional state after Theseus — LOCKED

This is one of the most important emotional decisions.

Westminster emerges:

- jubilant;
- relieved;
- whole;
- free of the nagging question that has plagued her whole life;
- genuinely happy.

The emotional beat is NOT:

> "Hmm, I seem to be a woman now."

It is not uncertain.

It is not experimental.

It is not the machine telling her who she is.

She knows.

The transformation gives her the physical reality she has consciously chosen.

The player discovers the truth at the same time they see how profoundly happy she is.

The key idea is:

> **Westminster finally gets to be happy.**

And:

> **She was there all along.**

The story is not fundamentally "Westminster gets turned into a woman."

It is closer to:

> Westminster finally gets to be the person she has always known herself to be.

# 8. Westminster personality before and after Theseus

Earlier notes used the terms "pre-op" and "post-op"; those terms are now outdated and should NOT be treated as preferred terminology. They were shorthand from an earlier stage of development.

The actual personality distinctions remain useful.

## Before Theseus

Westminster is:

- somewhat sullen;
- down;
- unfulfilled;
- generally moody;
- sarcastic;
- funny;
- prefers dark clothing;
- unadorned;
- sometimes overdoes the violence.

The important point is NOT that she is fundamentally a different person.

She is already Westminster.

Her humour is already there.

Her sarcasm is already there.

Her capacity for violence is already there.

But she is living in a state of persistent dissatisfaction / alienation.

## After Theseus

Westminster is:

- happy-go-lucky;
- likes bright things;
- likes pretty things;
- enjoys adornment;
- still sarcastic;
- still violent;
- but **FABULOUS with it**.

A loose tonal reference was Harley Quinn, but NOT as a model for literally making Westminster into Harley Quinn.

The useful elements are:

- gleeful;
- theatrical;
- colourful;
- dangerous;
- playful;
- fabulous.

The post-Theseus Westminster should still clearly be Westminster.

The change is primarily her baseline emotional state.

A useful formulation:

> Before: happiness occasionally breaks through the gloom.
>
> After: gloom occasionally breaks through the happiness.

Do NOT make her endlessly cheerful.

She can still:

- get angry;
- be sarcastic;
- have bad days;
- kill people;
- be recognisably Westminster.

The important change is that she is fundamentally comfortable and happy in herself.

# 9. Name — LOCKED

There was briefly an idea of Westminster changing her name to "Phoenix" after Theseus.

This has been rejected.

Reason:

- too on-the-nose;
- Project Theseus already carries enough symbolic weight;
- a dramatic new name risks implying she has become a different person;
- the story is about Westminster becoming herself, not becoming somebody new.

Therefore:

> **She remains Westminster.**

No Phoenix.

No additional rebirth metaphor is needed.

"Project Theseus" is already doing the symbolic heavy lifting.

---

# 10. Project Theseus — why the name works

The name was discovered before the complete concept existed and immediately felt exactly right.

The relevant philosophical reference is the **Ship of Theseus**.

The basic question:

> If every physical component of something is gradually replaced, at what point is it no longer the same thing?

Project Theseus applies that question to human beings.

West-Tek's researchers begin by asking:

> How much of a human body can we replace while preserving the identity / continuity of the person?

Then Westminster ultimately gives the project its most extreme answer.

Her body can be almost entirely reconstructed.

And yet:

> **Westminster remains Westminster.**

This is the thematic core.

# 11. The machine's original purpose — developing idea, now strongly favoured

The machine did NOT begin as "Project Theseus."

It began as a conventional West-Tek / U.S. military medical research programme.

The original problem should be practical:

> How do we return catastrophically wounded soldiers to service?

The strongest current origin idea:

The U.S. Army has a legendary soldier.

Possible rank:
- general;
- colonel;
- brigadier;
- etc.

The exact rank is not yet fixed.

He is:

- a military genius;
- crack shot;
- fearless;
- massively decorated;
- charismatic / legendary;
- exactly the sort of soldier the Army desperately does not want to lose.

Then a lucky enemy shot catastrophically destroys / vapourises his right arm.

The medical team can save his life.

But conventional medicine cannot restore the missing arm.

West-Tek is tasked with solving the problem.

A scientist reasons approximately:

> His DNA contains the instructions to make that arm.
>
> We just need to make a machine that can repeat those instructions.

This becomes the conceptual seed of the reconstruction technology.

The first goal is simply:

> **make him a new arm.**

The first machine is therefore not "Project Theseus."

It is a military battlefield-reconstruction / tissue-regeneration programme.

with the initials:

> **L. P. Roger**

> **"Trigger"**

This is considered delightful and should be retained.

# 13. The original battlefield-reconstruction progression

Current preferred progression:

1. Catastrophic injury to Trigger.
2. West-Tek tries to reconstruct his missing arm.
3. Arm reconstruction succeeds.
4. The Army realises this has enormous implications.
5. West-Tek begins experimenting with more complex tissue.
6. Legs.
7. Skin.
8. Bone.
9. Organs.
10. Increasingly comprehensive reconstruction.
11. Researchers realise they are no longer simply replacing individual body parts.
12. They are reconstructing an entire human biological system from a biological template.
13. This is where the project becomes philosophically different.
14. The name **Project Theseus** is eventually adopted.

The exact scientific chronology remains to be designed.

# 15. West-Tek lore and why Theseus is plausible

West-Tek is a major pre-War American defence contractor / research organisation.

Known Fallout associations include:

- advanced military technology;
- power armour research;
- weapons research;
- biological / chemical research;
- FEV / Forced Evolutionary Virus research;
- close involvement with the U.S. military.

West-Tek therefore already occupies exactly the sort of fictional space where an advanced military reconstruction programme is plausible.

Important approach:

> Theseus should fit into West-Tek's established history without needing to replace or rewrite known canon.

It should be a previously unknown / classified West-Tek programme operating in parallel with other research.

It does NOT need to be:

- secretly the origin of FEV;
- secretly responsible for another famous Fallout technology;
- a retcon of existing West-Tek lore.

Better:

> West-Tek was a huge military research organisation with many classified projects.
>
> Project Theseus was one of them.

The existing FEV history is useful mainly as precedent for:

- military pressure;
- compartmentalised research;
- increasingly extreme biological experimentation;
- West-Tek's ability to build advanced multidisciplinary technology;
- classified facilities.

Do not overconnect Theseus to FEV unless later development gives us a genuinely good reason.

---

# 16. West-Tek's research culture / thematic parallel

There is a useful parallel between FEV and Theseus.

FEV is effectively asking:

> Can we change what a human body is capable of being?

Theseus asks:

> Can we rebuild what a human body is?

They belong to the same broad West-Tek culture of increasingly ambitious biological research.

But they should remain separate programmes.

This makes Theseus feel like a natural part of the Fallout world rather than a contrived invention.

# 17. Project Theseus terminal / email chronology — LOCKED as approach

A series of terminal entries and/or internal messages is strongly favoured.

The idea is to let the player discover the history gradually rather than receiving one giant exposition dump.

"Email" is plausible enough for the era, but terminology can simply be "terminal messages", "internal correspondence", etc., depending on Fallout UI conventions.

The terminal sequence should begin with mundane military medical research and gradually become philosophical and extraordinary.

The previously developed examples were particularly liked and should be retained in spirit.

# 23. Whole-body reconstruction

The project eventually reaches the point where:

- skeletal system can be reconstructed;
- muscular system can be reconstructed;
- vascular system can be reconstructed;
- endocrine system can be reconstructed;

The machine can therefore reconstruct a complete biological body around the subject.

# 25. Project Theseus's ultimate question

The scientists initially ask:

> How much of a person can we replace while retaining the person?

Westminster eventually answers:

> **All of it.**

The machine can change:

- chromosomes;
- skeleton;
- organs;
- endocrine system;
- face;
- musculature;
- reproductive anatomy;
- essentially the entire physical body.

And Westminster remains Westminster.

The thematic answer is not:

> "A person is their body."

It is much closer to:

> **The person survived the body's complete transformation.**

And Westminster's response to the new body is not the machine determining her identity.

She already knew who she was.

# 30. ATWB / Tommi Starr as a relevant authorial precedent

The mod author's first major mod, ATWB, contains a deliberately elaborate quest to restore a radio station.

Quest chain:

1. Player receives quest.
2. Objective: investigate George Veek's house in Salem.
3. Finds:
   - photograph of Tommi's grandfather with guitar;
   - letter from Tommi to George saying he sent tapes of his show;
   - note from George's father saying he borrowed George's tape of rock songs and that Tommi has great stories.
4. Quest points to Wattz Electronics.
5. Finds note from Wayne Tournquist.
6. Wayne says he knows someone who can convert the tape into a programme that can broadcast via a transmitter and says he'll keep it safe at home in West Everett.
7. Player goes to West Everett.
8. Finds another note, partially in leetspeak, from paranoid programmer.
9. Programmer says the tape can be converted into instructions for a terminal connected to a powerful radio transmitter and will leave it at Med-Tek reception.
10. Player goes to Med-Tek.
11. Finds programme tape.
12. Quest points to WRVR.
13. Player reaches roof and uses custom terminal.
14. Runs transmitter-jacking programme.
15. 80 songs are copied to transmitter storage.
16. New radio frequency becomes available.
17. Player finally hears Tommi Starr / the radio show.
18. Reward: +1 CH for bringing rock music to the wasteland.

This quest is relevant because it demonstrates a preferred modding/storytelling style:

> The actual end product is less important than the archaeological journey that makes the player discover how it came to exist.

The creator particularly enjoyed the Wayne Tournquist connection and the use of existing Fallout lore/locations.

This suggests Project Theseus should not simply be:

> "Go to bunker, use machine."

Instead, the player should discover the history and provenance of Theseus through:

- terminals;
- messages;
- notes;
- environmental clues;
- existing Fallout lore;
- possibly existing locations;
- gradual revelation.

# 37. Current thematic architecture

Project Theseus starts with:

> "How do we rebuild a wounded soldier?"

It becomes:

> "How much of a human can we replace and still have the same person?"

It ends with Westminster demonstrating:

> "The person is still there."

The project was created to preserve a person's identity through catastrophic bodily reconstruction.

Westminster uses it to make her body finally correspond to the identity she already knows is hers.

The machine doesn't give her an identity.

It gives her the physical freedom to inhabit it.

# 38. Potential final irony / symmetry

The first great Theseus subject is likely:

> **L. P. Roger "Trigger"**

A decorated soldier whose right arm is destroyed.

The entire programme begins because the military refuses to accept that losing part of his body means losing his usefulness / identity.

West-Tek builds technology to give him back what was lost.

Over decades, the technology becomes capable of reconstructing an entire human body.

Then the Great War interrupts the project.

Two centuries later:

> **Westminster walks into the same technology voluntarily.**

The Army originally wanted to restore a soldier to the body they believed he should have.

Westminster uses the technology to choose the body she knows is hers.

This is potentially the thematic culmination of the entire project.

# 44. Current one-paragraph summary

Project Theseus is a secret pre-War West-Tek/U.S. military biomedical programme that evolves from an attempt to reconstruct catastrophically wounded soldiers into an effort to reconstruct entire human beings. It begins with the Army's legendary decorated soldier L. P. Roger "Trigger", whose right arm is destroyed in combat; West-Tek develops technology capable of recreating his lost limb from his biological template, then progressively expands the technology to organs, tissue and eventually whole-body reconstruction. As the scientists realise they are no longer simply repairing people, they adopt the name Project Theseus, invoking the question of whether a person remains the same person after every physical component has been replaced. By October 2077, whole-body reconstruction is ready for human trials, but the Great War interrupts the project. The facility survives, hidden beneath the Glowing Sea. In 2287, Westminster — who already knows she is trans and has consciously chosen to transition — is directed to Project Theseus. The player does not know what Westminster intends. She enters the machine, the player leaves, and after roughly 96–120 hours returns. Westminster emerges with a completely reconstructed female body, jubilant, relieved, whole and finally free of the question that has plagued her all her life. She remains Westminster: sarcastic, funny, violent and unmistakably herself, but now happy, colourful, adorned and fabulous. The player discovers only at that moment what the journey to Theseus was really about.
