# Project Theseus — Trigger and Origin
## Development checkpoint — 18 September 2026

This document records the development work and decisions made during the 18 September 2026 design session concerning Colonel Roger "Trigger" Lloyd and the origins of Project Theseus.

This is a development-history document, not a replacement for `CURRENT-STATE.md`. Durable decisions should subsequently be reflected in `CURRENT-STATE.md`.

---

# 1. Trigger — established character concept

## Colonel Roger "Trigger" Lloyd

Trigger is a legendary pre-War American special-operations operative.

His callsign is **Trigger**, and the callsign predates his injury.

He is a combination of:

- elite field operative;
- intelligence asset;
- covert assassin;
- reconnaissance operative;
- and, above all, an exceptional sniper.

His defining speciality is **making apparently impossible precision shots in impossible circumstances**.

He is not a conventional battlefield hero and does not have a famous conventional military victory associated with him.

His reputation comes from a long series of highly classified operations in which he is sent to eliminate exceptionally difficult targets.

The public record surrounding him is therefore likely to be sparse or deliberately misleading.

Within classified military circles, however, he is legendary.

The mental image is somewhere between Jason Bourne and James Bond, with the particular flavour of a highly experienced, extremely competent sniper. Clancy Brown is the current mental casting reference.

Trigger is not intended to be superhuman. His legend comes from extraordinary skill, experience, patience and judgement.

> Trigger is the man the people who send superheroes call when the job requires one shot.

---

# 2. The 2073 assassination mission

The current working incident takes place in **2073**, during the Sino-American War.

By this point, American leadership believes that the assassination of the Chinese head of government could potentially swing the war in America's favour.

The exact political reasoning does **not** need to be established at this stage.

It may involve succession, internal political instability, military strategy, negotiations, or some other classified consideration.

The important point is simply:

> The people making the decision believe that removing this one person could materially change the course of the war.

The mission is considered extraordinarily difficult.

The obvious question is:

> Who could possibly carry out such an assassination?

The answer:

> **Trigger.**

He is sent into China on a highly classified, deniable mission to assassinate the Chinese **Premier**.

"Premier" is currently preferred as the terminology for the Chinese head of government, although the exact Fallout-universe political terminology can be revisited if necessary.

---

# 3. Trigger fails

Trigger reaches his firing position.

He has the shot.

He **fails to kill the target**.

The precise reason for the failure is deliberately unresolved.

Several possibilities were discussed:

- an unknown Chinese technological defence;
- some kind of advanced Chinese security technology;
- a weapon malfunction;
- an environmental interruption;
- or something absurdly mundane.

The intentionally ridiculous possibility is that a chipmunk/squirrel appears in his scope at exactly the wrong moment.

The "stealth squirrel" joke is now part of the project's internal mythology but is **not canon**.

The important canon is:

> Trigger takes the impossible shot and misses.

He does not fail because he has suddenly become incompetent.

The circumstances of the failure remain to be determined.

---

# 4. The escape

Once the assassination attempt fails, Trigger has to escape China.

He is injured during the operation but remains sufficiently functional to reach the extraction point.

He is likely badly wounded, potentially including a gunshot wound to the leg, but Trigger is sufficiently tough and experienced to keep moving.

The extraction aircraft/vehicle is deliberately small enough to have approached without attracting attention.

It does not have the range to simply fly Trigger directly back to the United States.

The immediate destination is therefore likely to be **US-friendly territory in the region**, with Japan currently the leading conceptual fit.

The precise facility/base/hospital is TBD.

---

# 5. The loss of Trigger's arm

Trigger reaches the extraction point and gets aboard.

The aircraft immediately leaves at high speed.

Trigger is badly wounded and losing blood.

The pilot or crew applies emergency battlefield medical treatment to the catastrophic injury.

A stimpak may be part of the emergency treatment, since pre-War stimpaks are established technology, but a normal stimpak should not simply make a catastrophic traumatic amputation disappear.

The key incident occurs during the extraction itself.

Something on the ground — currently imagined as some form of **Chinese robotic/security technology** — catches Trigger's right arm at the shoulder.

Trigger is restrained by his harness.

The aircraft is already accelerating away.

The pilot cannot safely stop.

The result is catastrophic:

> **Trigger's right arm is ripped away.**

The arm is left behind.

There is nothing viable to recover or reattach.

Trigger survives, but his military career as an active field operator is effectively over.

The exact nature of the Chinese device remains TBD.

This should not be prematurely fixed.

---

# 6. The emergency call

Trigger is transported to an emergency medical facility in Japan or another nearby US-friendly location.

The immediate situation is critical.

A conceptual scene was developed in which the senior US leadership is informed while Trigger is still in transit:

> "No, Mr. Vice President. I'm afraid the mission failed. We had to evacuate the asset with extreme haste. He lost his right arm during the evacuation."

> "Trigger Lloyd lost his right arm? Are you fucking kidding me?"

> "No, sir. He's stable, but only just. We're en route to Japan. ETA eight minutes."

> "Tell me you at least have the arm."

> "No, sir."

> "...What?"

> "It was severed on the ground."

> "Christ."

> "But he's..."

> "He's the best in the world. We need him back in action ASAP."

> "We don't have the arm, sir."

> "Dammit."

> *click*

> *another call*

> "It's me. No. No, it didn't, and you'll hear about it soon enough. Shut up a second. Who do we have working on tissue regeneration?"

The exact dialogue is not yet final scripted material. Its purpose is to capture the intended dramatic logic.

The important decision is:

> The military response is not initially "how do we help Trigger recover?"

It is:

> **"How do we get Trigger back?"**

---

# 7. Why West-Tek is already positioned to respond

The answer to the Vice President's question is **West-Tek**.

This is important because Project Theseus should not appear from nowhere in 2073.

West-Tek is already conducting extensive research into biological growth and related medical/biological applications.

This research is connected to the development of the **Pan-Immunity Virion (PVP)**.

The exact relationship between all of the West-Tek research programmes remains to be developed, but the key established idea is:

> West-Tek has already spent an enormous amount of time researching cellular growth and biological behaviour before Trigger is injured.

---

# 8. The abandoned regenerative research

At some earlier point, West-Tek develops an experimental line/strain that produces an unexpected regenerative effect.

The effect is initially considered a **failure**.

It can cause tissue to regrow, but the growth is:

- uncontrolled;
- structurally abnormal;
- potentially carcinogenic;
- and medically useless in its initial form.

The researchers are not trying to create regeneration as such, or at least not successfully.

They are interested in other aspects of growth/cellular behaviour.

The anomalous regenerative effect is therefore treated as an undesirable side-effect.

The work is shelved.

However, the research is not destroyed.

The experimental data, records, samples and failed protocols remain somewhere within West-Tek's classified research infrastructure.

---

# 9. Trigger changes the meaning of the old research

After Trigger's injury, West-Tek is asked whether it can regenerate his missing arm.

The initial response may effectively be:

> "No. We can't regenerate an arm."

Then somebody remembers the old research.

The crucial realisation is:

> **West-Tek already knows how to make tissue grow. It simply doesn't know how to make it grow correctly.**

The old research therefore becomes unexpectedly valuable.

The scientists do not have to invent biological regeneration from scratch.

They have to learn how to **control** an existing regenerative phenomenon.

This becomes the central scientific problem of the early Theseus programme.

---

# 10. The central scientific problem

The project progresses through increasingly difficult problems:

1. controlled cellular growth;
2. preventing malignant or uncontrolled growth;
3. correct tissue structure;
4. correct spatial organisation;
5. vascularisation;
6. integration with existing tissue;
7. nervous-system integration;
8. regeneration of increasingly complex structures;
9. coordinated regeneration of a complete limb;
10. increasingly extensive biological reconstruction;
11. eventually, complete human reconstruction.

The important conceptual transition is:

> **"Can we make tissue grow?"**

becoming:

> **"Can we make it grow the right thing, in the right place, with the right structure, and then make it stop?"**

This provides a natural scientific progression from the old West-Tek research to Project Theseus.

---

# 11. The origin of Theseus

Project Theseus is therefore not invented as a single breakthrough.

It is **refined**.

The broad progression is:

**West-Tek growth research**

→ anomalous regenerative effect

→ regenerative research dismissed as pathological/unusable

→ Trigger loses his arm in 2073

→ military asks whether the phenomenon can be controlled

→ old research is recovered

→ regeneration becomes controllable

→ complex tissues become possible

→ limb regeneration becomes possible

→ increasingly ambitious biological reconstruction

→ the project evolves beyond Trigger

→ complete human regeneration/reconstruction becomes the objective

→ Project Theseus.

The key thematic idea is:

> **They didn't set out to create Theseus. They wanted their sniper back.**

A second useful formulation:

> **Theseus wasn't invented. It was refined.**

And perhaps the most concise version of the entire origin:

> **The scientists didn't discover how to make life grow. They discovered how to make it stop growing wrong.**

---

# 12. Trigger and the eventual significance of Theseus

Trigger is therefore the immediate human reason that the old regenerative research is revived.

He is not necessarily the first person experimented on.

He is not currently established as the first successful regenerated limb.

Those details remain to be determined.

What is established is that:

> **Trigger's injury causes the military to take previously unwanted regenerative research seriously.**

The project eventually grows beyond the original objective.

By 2077, it has become Project Theseus and is capable of approaching complete human regeneration.

The current canon remains:

> **Theseus has never successfully performed a complete human regeneration before Westminster.**

Westminster will eventually become the first complete human subject.

# 13. Things deliberately left unresolved

## Trigger's failed shot

Why did Trigger miss the Chinese Premier?

Possibilities discussed include:

- unknown Chinese technology;
- advanced defensive systems;
- weapon malfunction;
- environmental interference;
- something completely mundane;
- the deliberately comic "stealth squirrel" possibility.

No answer is currently canon.

## The Chinese device

What exactly catches Trigger's arm?

Possibilities include:

- robot;
- autonomous security system;
- advanced Chinese military machine;
- another piece of technology.

No final choice has been made.

## Trigger's extraction

The exact aircraft, route, extraction site, Japanese destination and medical facility remain TBD.

## Trigger's treatment

The exact pre-War emergency medical technology used to stabilise him remains TBD.

## The old West-Tek research

The exact name of the abandoned project/strain, when it was discovered, what it was originally intended to do, and the precise nature of its regenerative pathology remain TBD.

## Trigger's eventual fate

No post-War fate has been established.

The previously discussed possibility of Trigger surviving as a ghoul has been **discarded completely** and should not be revived as a current or WHAT IF idea unless explicitly reconsidered in future.

---

# 14. Development principle

The Theseus backstory should be built as **archaeology rather than exposition**.

The player does not need to be told the entire story.

The facility should contain fragments from different stages of the research.

Early material may appear unrelated to Theseus.

Later discoveries reveal connections between:

- West-Tek growth research;
- the abandoned regenerative phenomenon;
- Trigger;
- military funding;
- the increasingly ambitious research;
- and eventually Project Theseus itself.

The ideal effect is that the player gradually reconstructs a history that existed for centuries before they arrived.

The full story should reward investigation without requiring the player to discover every piece.

---

# 15. Current next step

The **conceptual evolution** from "regrow Trigger's arm" to full human regeneration is established.

What remains to be developed is the **specific 2073–2077 research chronology**: the experiments, breakthroughs, failures and discoveries that take the scientists from the initial revived regenerative research to the completed Project Theseus system.

Specifically:

- What exactly was the old regenerative phenomenon?
- What was West-Tek originally trying to achieve with the relevant growth research?
- How did the unwanted regenerative effect first appear?
- Why was it considered a failure?
- Who discovers or remembers it after Trigger is injured?
- What is the first practical application of the revived research?
- What are the major breakthroughs between 2073 and 2077?
- What failures and setbacks occur?
- At what point does the work cease to be merely "the Trigger project" and become Project Theseus?
- What finally makes complete human regeneration possible?

The key constraint is that the progression should feel like **four years of increasingly successful scientific problem-solving**, not one miraculous invention.
