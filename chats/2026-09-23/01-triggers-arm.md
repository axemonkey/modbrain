# IMPORTANT: New Canon Locked This Session

The following material was explicitly agreed as canon during this session.

## BattlefieldBuddy

West-Tek's original regeneration technology was developed as a battlefield first-aid system, later known as **BattlefieldBuddy**.

Its original purpose was rapid repair of serious but non-fatal battlefield injuries.

The basic concept:

- The device analyses a wound.
- It uses extremely advanced microsurgery combined with controlled radiation.
- The radiation stimulates cellular mutation/regeneration.
- The device shapes and directs the resulting regeneration so that damaged tissue repairs itself.
- A serious injury that would normally require surgery and weeks or months of healing can potentially be repaired in minutes.
- A severed finger or similar body part can potentially be reattached/regenerated **provided the original part is recovered and viable**.
- At this stage, BattlefieldBuddy cannot create a completely missing body part.

The important technological boundary is:

> BattlefieldBuddy can make the body repair what is still there. It cannot initially make the body grow something that is completely missing.

The technology therefore represents accelerated and controlled healing rather than true tissue fabrication.

---

## Why BattlefieldBuddy Was Shelved

The regenerative process was difficult to control.

The induced cellular mutation could occasionally go "off-script", producing tissue that did not correspond to the actual injury.

One particularly memorable incident involved a soldier who suffered a bullet wound through the thigh muscle.

The device successfully repaired the thigh.

However, it also caused **the soldier's own ear to grow at the site of the wound**.

The ear is not random or generic.

It is recognisably and specifically **that soldier's ear**.

This is important.

The failure demonstrates that the regenerative process is somehow accessing an internal biological/informational blueprint of the individual.

The researchers initially understand this as a problem with controlling cellular differentiation.

Much later, the significance becomes clearer:

> The machine is not merely reading DNA. It is somehow accessing an intrinsic blueprint of the person.

The underlying nature of this blueprint is deliberately mysterious.

It is not necessarily something the scientists would call a "soul", and we should avoid prematurely defining its metaphysics.

Possible working language includes:

- intrinsic blueprint
- intrinsic template
- identity pattern
- deeper biological/informational template

But beneath the scientific terminology is the possibility that the technology is interacting with something closer to the person's essential identity or soul.

This is a foundational concept for the later Theseus technology.

# Trigger and the Return of BattlefieldBuddy

Trigger is currently Colonel Roger "Trigger" Lloyd, a legendary pre-War covert operative/sniper.

In the working 2073 history, Trigger is sent into China to assassinate the Chinese Premier.

He fails to kill the target and escapes.

During extraction, his right arm is catastrophically torn off at the shoulder.

The military subsequently asks West-Tek the obvious question:

> Who do we have working on tissue regeneration?

Someone remembers the old BattlefieldBuddy research.

The project is pulled off the shelf and receives enormous military funding and resources.

The military's requirement is simple:

> Make Trigger's arm grow back.

This is the event that turns the abandoned battlefield medicine programme into the research lineage that eventually becomes Project Theseus.

---

# Animal Limb Regeneration

Once the old BattlefieldBuddy research is revived, the researchers work on the fundamental problem created by Trigger's injury:

> What happens when the tissue that needs to be repaired no longer exists?

They conduct increasingly ambitious animal experiments.

These experiments involve deliberately amputating limbs from test animals and attempting to regenerate the missing limbs.

The experiments are morally awful, deliberately reflecting the Fallout setting and pre-War scientific culture.

Eventually they succeed.

The animals can reliably regenerate their own missing limbs.

This establishes that the researchers have crossed an important technological boundary:

> They are no longer merely accelerating healing. They can reconstruct missing biological structures.

The animal trials eventually become reliable enough that the researchers consider attempting the procedure on a human being.

There are no suitable human volunteers willing to be the first subject.

Trigger volunteers.

---

# Trigger's First Human Regeneration Trial

This is **locked canon**.

Trigger volunteers to be the first human subject for deliberate limb regeneration.

His motivation is consistent with his established character: he is the man whose injury started the research, and he is willing to put himself on the line rather than ask someone else to take the risk.

The first human procedure appears to work.

An arm is successfully regenerated.

But there is something profoundly wrong with it.

It is not Trigger's present-day adult arm.

It is **an earlier version of Trigger's own arm**.

The exact age is not necessarily permanently fixed yet, although the working version is approximately ten years old.

The arm is anatomically correct and unmistakably Trigger's.

It is not a random mutation.

It is not generic childhood anatomy.

It is specifically Roger Lloyd's arm from an earlier point in his own life.

---

# The Intrinsic Blueprint

The first human trial provides the researchers with an extraordinary clue.

The animal trials could demonstrate that an individual's missing limb could be reconstructed.

Only a human subject can reveal that the reconstruction process is doing something much stranger.

The machine has not simply reconstructed:

> Trigger's current arm.

It has somehow accessed **a previous version of Trigger**.

The researchers initially regard this as a failure.

But the failure teaches them what the machine is actually doing.

The regenerative system appears to be accessing the individual's deeper intrinsic blueprint rather than simply copying their current biological state.

The important conceptual distinction is:

> The blueprint is not a record of the body.

> It is something closer to a record of the person.

The machine does not merely ask:

> "What does this person's body look like?"

It is somehow interacting with:

> "Who is this person?"

---

# Why the Younger Trigger?

The exact age is still flexible.

The current idea is that the regenerated arm corresponds to a period when Roger was **at his happiest**.

The important point is therefore not necessarily "ten years old".

It is:

> The younger version of Roger represented by the regenerated arm was associated with a particularly strong/happy period in his life.

A possible interpretation is that something from this period became especially strongly represented in his intrinsic blueprint.

The language used during discussion was that this younger version of Roger somehow **"rises to the surface" of his soul/blueprint** during regeneration.

This is currently a conceptual model rather than a scientifically defined mechanism.

The exact childhood history that explains why this particular version of Roger is so strongly represented remains to be developed.

# Trigger's Childhood — UNRESOLVED

A possible backstory was discussed but **not locked as canon**.

One possibility:

- Trigger's mother dies when he is young.
- His father is already a career soldier.
- His father raises Roger alone.
- He loves his son but only knows how to express responsibility through military-style discipline and strictness.
- Roger's childhood therefore becomes highly structured and emotionally restrained.
- The loss of his mother leaves a deep psychological/emotional mark.
- His eventual military career may be influenced by this upbringing, but should not necessarily be reduced to a simple "trauma made him join the military" explanation.

Another possibility was that Trigger's parents separated when he was young, with the separation becoming a major formative trauma.

Neither version is currently locked.

What *is* locked is the underlying concept:

> There was a period in Trigger's early life associated with profound happiness, and that version of Roger is somehow strongly represented in the intrinsic blueprint.

The exact age and the specific childhood event should be determined later.

# The Second Trigger Attempt

This is **locked canon**.

The researchers realise that the first regeneration did not simply fail.

It revealed that they had been asking the wrong question.

They had assumed they needed to reproduce Trigger's current physical state.

Instead, the machine was responding to the deeper identity blueprint.

Once they understand this, they modify/refine the procedure so that the reconstruction is anchored to **Trigger as he is now**.

They perform the procedure again.

The second attempt succeeds.

Trigger receives a correctly reconstructed **adult arm**.

This is a crucial milestone in the development of the technology.

The first human attempt is therefore simultaneously:

- a failure from the immediate practical perspective;
- an extraordinary scientific discovery;
- the event that reveals the nature of the intrinsic blueprint;
- and the key insight that allows the second attempt to succeed.

The important causal chain is:

> First human attempt → younger Trigger's arm → investigation → discovery of the intrinsic blueprint → understanding how to anchor reconstruction to the present identity → successful adult arm.

The first attempt should therefore not be treated as a pointless failed experiment.

**The failure is the breakthrough.**

# Trigger and Theseus

Trigger's successful adult arm regeneration does **not** violate the established Westminster-first rule.

By October 2077:

- BattlefieldBuddy can repair serious wounds.
- Regeneration has become controllable.
- Missing limbs can be reconstructed.
- The researchers have begun understanding the intrinsic blueprint.
- Increasingly complex biological structures can be reconstructed.
- This knowledge eventually leads toward organs and increasingly comprehensive reconstruction.
- The research eventually becomes the programme known as **Project Theseus**.

However:

> **There has never been a complete human-body reconstruction.**

Trigger is not a full Theseus subject.

Nobody has ever undergone complete human reconstruction before Westminster.

Westminster remains the first complete human subject.

This distinction is essential and must not be lost.

# Project Theseus Naming

The mod/story itself remains **unnamed**.

"Project Theseus" refers only to the in-universe West-Tek/U.S. military research programme.

The research was **not initially called Project Theseus**.

The work begins as battlefield regeneration/BattlefieldBuddy research.

The name "Theseus" emerges later as the scope of the research expands toward complete human reconstruction and the question of how much of a person can be replaced while they remain the same person.

Therefore:

- Mod/story: unnamed.
- Early research: BattlefieldBuddy / earlier West-Tek regeneration work.
- Later research programme: Project Theseus.

Do not retroactively call the entire research history "Project Theseus".

The scientific progression remains broadly:

battlefield wound repair
→ accelerated regeneration
→ controlled regeneration
→ replacement tissue
→ animal limb regeneration
→ human limb regeneration
→ increasingly complex structures
→ organs
→ increasingly extensive reconstruction
→ complete human reconstruction
→ Project Theseus

The philosophical question underlying the later programme is the Ship of Theseus:

> How much of a human being can be replaced while the person remains the same person?

The intrinsic blueprint provides the eventual scientific/mystical mechanism by which the technology can distinguish **the person** from merely **the current body**.

# Westminster

Previously established canon remains unchanged.

Westminster already knows she is trans before reaching Theseus.

The player does not initially know this.

She consciously chooses the procedure.

The machine does not diagnose her identity or decide that she is a woman.

Instead, the technology reconstructs her according to the deeper intrinsic blueprint of who she is.

The important sequence is:

> Westminster knows who she is → Westminster chooses the procedure → Theseus reconstructs her body according to that identity.

The technology therefore provides physical reconstruction rather than identity discovery.

The eventual transformation remains a genuine biological reconstruction, not cosmetic surgery.

The complete procedure takes roughly 96–120 hours.

There has never previously been a successful complete human trial.

Westminster is genuinely the first.
